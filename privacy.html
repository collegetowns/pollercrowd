Privacy

What Poller Crowd keeps, and where things go

Poller Crowd is built to collect as little as possible. People joining don’t make accounts or give names, answers aren’t saved on any server, and results stay on the presenter’s own computer. This page explains exactly what happens, in plain language.

People who joinTeachers & presentersWhere data goesNot for gradingFor organizations

People who join (students, audience members)

  • No accounts, names or emails. People join with a QR code or room code and pick a cartoon buddy. A nickname is optional and only shows on quiz leaderboards; the app asks for a fun name, not a real one.
  • Answers are anonymous. The presenter sees buddies, not people.
  • Please don’t type personal information. Phones remind people not to put names or personal details in word cloud or Q&A answers. The filter replaces rude words with symbols like #!*&, but it can’t spot personal details, so presenters can also hold typed answers for approval, hide anything, and remove someone from the room.
  • Nothing is saved on a server. Answers travel from the phone to the presenter’s screen through a live connection and aren’t kept there (details below).
  • What the phone keeps: a random ID, the chosen buddy and nickname, and the person’s own answers, so reloading the page doesn’t lose them. These stay in the phone’s browser and are deleted after a week.

Teachers and presenters

  • Question sets and results are saved only in the presenter’s browser, on that computer. They leave it only if the presenter downloads an export (spreadsheet, summary or PowerPoint) or a question set file.
  • What a session’s results contain: each answer, linked to a buddy and an optional nickname. The presenter’s screen sees a scrambled (one-way hashed) version of each phone’s random ID, never the ID itself.
  • Past sessions are deleted automatically after 30 days. Presenters can delete them sooner from the home screen or Settings.
  • Photos added to questions stay in the presenter’s browser and are only shown on the big screen.
  • Exports are the presenter’s to look after. Once downloaded, treat them like any other work file.

Anyone with a room’s code or link can join it, and the room’s live updates (mostly what’s on the big screen) aren’t encrypted beyond the normal https connection. Updates from the presenter are digitally signed, so phones ignore fakes. Don’t put anything private in a question.

The shared connection runs on College Towns’ own Supabase project. People or organizations who use their own Supabase or Firebase project, or host their own copy, control that connection themselves, including which part of the world it runs in.

Not for grading

Poller Crowd is for checking understanding, sparking discussion and making a class or talk more fun. It isn’t a test or gradebook tool, and results shouldn’t be used for grades:

  • Nothing confirms who answered. Anyone with the code can join, people can share a phone or join twice, and nicknames are made up.
  • Answers can go missing. A weak connection, a phone going to sleep or a reloaded page can drop an answer, and there’s no record to appeal to.
  • Quiz race timing depends on the connection. Points for speed reward a fast network as much as a fast thinker. That’s fine for a game, not for a grade.
  • Records aren’t kept. Results live in one browser and are deleted after 30 days.
  • It keeps privacy simple. Because answers aren’t tied to identified people, Poller Crowd stays out of the records business. Using it for grades would change that.

For graded quizzes, use your school’s or university’s learning platform (such as Canvas, Moodle, Google Classroom or Blackboard).

Privacy laws

Poller Crowd is designed to collect as little as possible: answers are anonymous, aren’t kept on a server, and aren’t meant for grading or official records. Which laws apply depends on where you are and who’s in the room. Examples include data-protection laws such as the GDPR in the EU and UK, and student-privacy laws such as FERPA and COPPA in the United States. Some points that come up in reviews:

  • IP addresses are seen by the website host and the live-connection service as part of normal internet traffic. Some laws, including the GDPR, treat them as personal data.
  • Data location: to keep connection data in a particular region, use your own Supabase or Firebase project set to that region (see the setup guide).
  • Student records: anonymous, short-lived poll results generally aren’t records about an identified student. That can change if a presenter links answers to named people or keeps them as records.
  • Children: for participants under 13 (or your country’s age of digital consent), schools usually decide whether a tool may be used, under their own policies.

We’re not lawyers, and nothing here is legal advice. Please follow your school’s, university’s or organization’s approval process before using Poller Crowd with students or other participants.
