Privacy

What Poller Crowd keeps, and where things go

Poller Crowd is built to collect as little as possible. Students don’t make accounts or give names, answers aren’t saved on any server, and results stay on the teacher’s own computer. This page explains exactly what happens, in plain language.

StudentsTeachersWhere data goesNot for gradingFor schools

Students

  • No accounts, names or emails. Students join with a QR code or room code and pick a cartoon buddy. A nickname is optional and only shows on quiz leaderboards; the app asks for a fun name, not a real one.
  • Answers are anonymous. The teacher sees buddies, not people.
  • Please don’t type personal information. Phones remind students not to put names or personal details in word cloud or Q&A answers. The filter replaces rude words with symbols like #!*&, but it can’t spot personal details, so teachers can also hold typed answers for approval, hide anything, and remove a student from the room.
  • Nothing is saved on a server. Answers travel from the phone to the teacher’s screen through a live connection and aren’t kept there (details below).
  • What the phone keeps: a random ID, the chosen buddy and nickname, and the student’s own answers, so reloading the page doesn’t lose them. These stay in the phone’s browser and are deleted after a week.

Teachers

  • Question sets and results are saved only in the teacher’s browser, on that computer. They leave it only if the teacher downloads an export (spreadsheet, summary or PowerPoint) or a question set file.
  • What a session’s results contain: each answer, linked to a buddy and an optional nickname. The teacher’s screen sees a scrambled (one-way hashed) version of each phone’s random ID, never the ID itself.
  • Past sessions are deleted automatically after 30 days. Teachers can delete them sooner from the home screen or Settings.
  • Photos added to questions stay in the teacher’s browser and are only shown on the big screen.
  • Exports are the teacher’s to look after. Once downloaded, treat them like any other class file.

Anyone with a room’s code or link can join it, and the room’s live updates (mostly what’s on the big screen) aren’t encrypted beyond the normal https connection. Updates from the teacher are digitally signed, so phones ignore fakes. Don’t put anything private in a question.

Teachers or schools who use their own Supabase or Firebase project, or host their own copy, control that connection themselves.

Not for grading

Poller Crowd is for checking understanding, sparking discussion and making class more fun. It isn’t a test or gradebook tool, and results shouldn’t be used for grades:

  • Nothing confirms who answered. Anyone with the code can join, students can share a phone or join twice, and nicknames are made up.
  • Answers can go missing. A weak connection, a phone going to sleep or a reloaded page can drop an answer, and there’s no record to appeal to.
  • Quiz race timing depends on the connection. Points for speed reward a fast network as much as a fast thinker. That’s fine for a game, not for a grade.
  • Records aren’t kept. Results live in one browser and are deleted after 30 days.
  • It keeps privacy simple. Because answers aren’t tied to identified students, Poller Crowd stays out of the student-records business. Using it for grades would change that.

For graded quizzes, use your school’s learning platform (Canvas, Google Classroom, Moodle and so on).

FERPA and COPPA

Poller Crowd is designed to avoid creating student records. Answers are anonymous, aren’t kept on a server, and aren’t meant for grading. Under FERPA, education records are records directly related to a student and kept by the school. Anonymous, short-lived class poll results generally don’t fit that, but that can change if a teacher links answers to named students or keeps them as records. For students under 13, schools usually decide whether a classroom tool may be used under their own COPPA process. We’re not lawyers, and nothing here is legal advice. Please follow your school’s or district’s approval process before using Poller Crowd with students.
